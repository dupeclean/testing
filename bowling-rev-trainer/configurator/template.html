Measured as a pro shop measures it: from the centre of the grip (midway between the thumb hole and the middle of the two finger holes) for a thumb-in grip, or from the middle of the two finger holes for a two-handed, no-thumb ball. Over first, along the line across the grip, then up. Leave the box unticked if the sheet has no PAP: the ball then uses the common 5 1/2" over, 1/2" up.

Sizes and spans take the drill sheet's own notation: 55/64, 1 1/32, 4-1/2 or decimals. Span is the near edge of the thumb hole to the near edge of each finger hole. Zero pitch aims the hole at the ball's center.

Ball: all three holes, at the sheet's spans and pitches, drilled into a full ball you can release on the floor. The thumb hole is blind (its depth is set below), like a real ball.

Spinner: only the two finger hole sizes and the bridge are used. Both holes are drilled straight at the ball's center, so the spans and pitches on this sheet do not apply to the spinner. Switch to Puck to use them. An optional humming chamber (under Options) makes the spinner hum once the finger lift spins it fast enough.

Which hole is which: read the sheet with the thumb hole at the bottom, as it is drawn. For a right hand the left finger hole is the middle finger and the right hole is the ring finger (a left hand is the mirror image). Enter each hole's numbers on the row with the matching side, whatever the sheet calls it.

Pitch is in inches, as drilled. Forward (+): the inner end of the hole leans toward the middle of the grip: the thumb hole toward the fingers, the finger holes toward the thumb. Reverse (−): the other way. Lateral (+): toward the right side of the sheet for a right hand (toward the left for a left hand); − is the other side. The blue lines in the 3D view show where each hole points.

Oval holes

Leave at 0 for round holes. For an oval hole enter the sheet's hole size on the Drill sheet row (the short way across, the drilled size) and the oval size here (the long way across). The angle is where the long axis points on the sheet: 0 = straight up the sheet along the thumb-to-fingers line, positive = turned toward the thumb side of the sheet (to the left for a right hand, to the right for a left hand), negative toward the little-finger side. Hold the real ball with the finger holes at 12 o'clock and check the preview leans the same way before printing.

Options

The hum is a threshold: it starts when the finger lift spins the spinner fast enough and lasts as long as the spin stays above that speed, so you hear how hard the lift was and how long it carried. The wrist-cord tunnel is left out when the chamber is on (it would cross the chamber). Not yet tuned by test prints: the slot count and angle change the starting speed and the note.

Rev Ball

A release trainer: kneel, swing low, let it go onto the floor along a strip of tape and watch what it does. Thumb out first and fingers lifting makes it roll and curve off the line; a dump rolls straight and short; a hung-up thumb pops it up. Printed solid-looking but sliced light (3 walls, 5% infill, about 500 g at 200 mm). It needs supports under its lower half; sand the scar smooth. A second colour makes the spin easy to see: the slicer swaps filament at the heights FitSlice gives you, so a small spot above the grip holes (or the full cap) and a band come out in the other colour; FitSlice places them from the layout, the band clear of the bullseyes, and the .3mf in the download carries the colours already assigned. The axis target (dots and rings, set below) marks the axis your release should spin about: spin it right and the bullseye stands still while the rest of the ball blurs. Its contrasting plugs and rings come in their own file.

The dots sit at the PAP on the drill sheet above (or the common 5 1/2" over, 1/2" up when the sheet has none). The plugs, rings and track arcs export as one second-colour file.
Sizer plate

A quick flat print with labeled test bores. Try each bore to the first joint; the one that is snug all around with no squeeze needed to hang on is your size on this printer. Each bore is engraved with its diameter in millimetres. To use what you found: switch the units to mm, set “Hole sizes come from” to The printed sizer plate (that sets the print allowance to 0, because the plate already includes the printer's effect), then type the engraved numbers as your hole sizes on the Puck or Spinner.

Release gate

The clack that says a release worked. Loose pins stand in shallow cups on base segments linked across a strip of tape on the floor, 1.5 to 2 m from where the ball is let go. The ball knocks over the pin where it crosses, about a second after the release. Decide beforehand which pin a correct release should topple: the centre pin for straight, heavy forward roll; one or two pins over for a hook. The wrong pin falling says which way the release missed. Pins sit the ball's diameter + 10 mm apart, so the ball never reaches more than two neighbours. This file holds one segment and one pin: print 3 or 5 copies, flat, no supports.

# Vent whistle: the thumb-first test

**What this settles.** When a snug thumb leaves a blind hole fast it pulls a vacuum behind it.
That is the "thumb pop" bowlers listen for, and why pro shops drill vent holes. If that gulp of
air can sound a small printed whistle, the Rev Ball and the puck can play the release order out
loud: the thumb note first, then the finger note, with no moving parts, no springs, nothing bought.
Whether the air flow is enough is not knowable from a model. This coupon answers it in one print.

**What you print.** `whistle_coupon.stl`, one block 150 x 60 x 50 mm with five blind holes.
PETG or PLA, 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 15% infill, holes facing up, no supports (every channel
inside has a 45-degree roof). About 1 h 20 min.

| Hole | What it is | Whistle |
|---|---|---|
| A | thumb hole (your 25/32" + allowance) | 3 mm orifices, 12 x 10 mm cavity |
| B | thumb hole | 2.5 mm orifices, 10 x 8 mm cavity (should be higher pitched) |
| C | thumb hole | 3.5 mm orifices, 14 x 12 mm cavity (lower, freer) |
| D | thumb hole | plain 3 mm vent, no cavity: the control |
| F | middle finger hole (21/32" + allowance) | 2 mm orifices, 8 x 6 mm cavity |

The whistles open on the long face marked "whistles this side". Keep that face clear.

**How to test.**

1. Tape the thumb hole snug, as you would on a ball. The seal is the whole point: a loose
   thumb lets the air in round the thumb instead of through the whistle.
2. Put the thumb in to the second joint, hold the block in the other hand, and pull the thumb
   out fast, the way it leaves at the bottom of the swing. Listen. Do it ten times per hole.
3. Then F with the middle finger, fingertip to the first joint.
4. Write down, for each letter: a clear note, a hiss, or nothing, and roughly how loud (would
   you hear it on a carpet at home; would you hear it in a bowling centre).

**How to read it.**

- A, B or C gives a clear note and D only hisses: the whistle works. The one with the clearest
  note goes into the ball and the puck as an option, with a smaller, higher one on the finger
  holes. A good release then sounds as a low note, a gap, then a high note; a dump as one chord.
- Everything hisses, nothing sings: the air flow is too small or too brief. We drop the idea
  and say so, rather than ship a quiet feature.
- F is the harder case (a fingertip moves less air). If the thumb sings and F does not, the
  thumb note alone still tells you "thumb out first" against the feel of the fingers lifting.

**What it is not.** It cannot sense the hand; it reports the order the holes empty, which is the
thing itself. It needs a snug fit, which a trainer should have anyway.

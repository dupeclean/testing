## 1. Positioning statement

For one-handed, thumb-in league bowlers who already have a pro-shop fit, RollRead is the made-to-order training aid built from their own drill sheet on a slice with the curvature of a regulation 8.5-inch ball, so they can practice the grip, the thumb exit and the finger lift anywhere. Unlike generic one-size practice balls and S/M/L wrist braces, it is built from the bowler's fit. To our knowledge, it is the first trainer that uses the bowler's actual drill sheet.

**Category name we use:** custom-fit release trainer.
**What we never call it:** a ball, a mini ball, a regulation anything, a rev booster.

**Brand in one line:** Your drill sheet, off the ball.

**Brand in ten seconds:** It's the part of your ball your hand touches, printed with your exact holes, so you can practice your release anywhere.

**The three things the brand stands on**

1. Fit is a fact. We build the model from the drill sheet in 1/64-inch steps, with both spans, every pitch, right or left hand. We can check the printed holes against the bowler's own sheet on a bench with a sizer plate.
2. The hand is what we train. Coaches agree the thumb must leave first and a relaxed hand creates more revs. Our six drills are designed to practice those two things, in the bowler's own hole geometry.
3. We say what we know. No rev-rate numbers, no average numbers, no hook numbers until our pilot has measured them. A 30-day fit guarantee backs the fit itself: if the printed holes miss the sheet, we reprint.

## 2. Brand story (founder's voice)

I bowl one-handed with my thumb in the ball, the way most of the league bowlers I know still do. My pro shop measured my hand to a sixty-fourth of an inch, picked my spans and my pitches, and drilled them into a ball I throw a few nights a week. That fit is the most personal piece of equipment I own, and it lives in exactly one place: a fifteen-pound ball I cannot hold at my desk, carry in a backpack, or drop-catch in my living room.

The coaches I have listened to agree the release comes down to the same things. Keep the hand relaxed. Get the thumb out first and clean. Let the fingers do the lifting. Each of those is a hand skill, and I believe each is best practiced with the hand sitting in its own holes. A generic practice ball with one-size holes does not match your hand. A wrist brace holds the wrist for you.

So I asked a simple question. What if you could take the drill sheet off the ball?

A bowling ball is 8.5 inches across. The part your hand touches is a small patch of that sphere. Print that patch at the real curvature, aim the holes at the ball's center the way a zero-pitch hole is drilled, apply your pitches, and your thumb and fingers oppose each other as they do on your ball. It weighs about 300 grams in PETG at 20% infill. It goes in a bag.

That is RollRead. It copies your drill sheet. It does not change your fit, and it does not promise you a number. It gives you your grip, anywhere, so you can practice what coaches say matters.

## 3. Voice and tone

**Voice in three words:** plain, exact, confident.

We write like a good pro-shop operator talks: short sentences, real numbers, no hedging on what we can measure, no bluffing on what we cannot. The reader already knows what a span is. We respect that.

### Tone by situation

- **Product pages and ads:** confident and specific. Numbers we can measure, the ten-second line, one call to action.
- **FAQ and support:** candid. If we do not know, we say "we do not know, and here is what we are doing to find out."
- **Pro shops and coaches:** peer to peer. They know fit better than we do. We talk about the sheet, the margin and the counter, and we never tell them how to fit a bowler.
- **Parents:** calm and practical. Weight, safety, supervision, price.
- **Press:** facts in boilerplate order. No adjectives the reporter would delete.

### Quotes

A coach, creator or shop is quoted only after they have said the words, in writing or on recording, and agreed to the use. Until then the layout carries `[QUOTE PLACEHOLDER — name]`. We do not write quotes for people and ask them to approve. A quote from anyone who received a free unit, a code or payment carries the matching disclosure line from this section beside it. We do not run quotes that describe a performance result until the pilot shows what is typical.

## 7. FAQ

**1. What is RollRead?**
A made-to-order training aid for one-handed, thumb-in bowlers. The Rev Puck is a slice with the curvature of a regulation 8.5-inch ball, 3D printed in PETG and built from your drill sheet, so you can practice your grip and release off the lane. The Rev Spinner is a fingers-only disc for finger-lift practice. The Fit Kit is a sizer plate for confirming your hole sizes.

**2. What do I need to order?**
Your drill sheet: thumb, middle and ring hole sizes, your middle and ring spans, and the forward/reverse and lateral pitch for each hole. Your pro shop has it on file. Type it into the free configurator at https://claude.ai/artifact/1fiDeAkPe9RPJSyBWruE2p and your model appears in 3D. Each part is printed to order, and we quote the ship date when you order.

**3. I don't have a drill sheet.**
Ask your pro shop for it. It is your fit, and the shop that drilled your ball should have it on file. If you bowl with a house ball or have never been fitted, start with the $24 Fit Kit, which helps you find your hole sizes, and have a pro shop measure your spans and pitches. The $24 is credited toward your trainer.

**4. How exact is it?**
The configurator models hole sizes in 1/64-inch steps, both spans, and forward/reverse and lateral pitch per hole, right or left hand, on an 8.5-inch ball radius. Its geometry matches our CAD source to 0.1 mm. The printed part depends on the printer. Printed holes come out slightly small on 3D printers, so the configurator adds a print allowance, and the Fit Kit sizer is printed on the same printer and material as your order so the bore that fits you is the bore we print. We have not yet published measurements of printed holes, so we do not claim an exact printed fit.

**5. Will it feel like my ball?**
The curvature is the real 8.5-inch radius and the holes are aimed at the ball's center the way a zero-pitch hole is drilled, with your pitches applied, so your thumb and fingers oppose each other exactly as on your ball. Finish the fit the way you would on a ball: a wrap of bowler's tape in the thumb if you want it snugger. If the printed holes miss your sheet, we reprint. We have not yet run a blind feel test with bowlers, so we describe the geometry and leave the verdict to you.

**6. What is the Fit Kit?**
A flat printed plate with test bores centered on your expected sizes in 1/64-inch steps, labels engraved, plus a roll of bowler's tape and a fitting card. The right bore is snug all around at the first joint with no squeeze needed to hang on. If you are unsure, ask your pro shop. It costs $24, credited toward a trainer.

**7. What is the difference between the Rev Puck and the Rev Spinner?**
The Rev Puck has all three holes from your sheet, a weight pocket and a wrist-cord tunnel, and is used for holds, thumb pops and shadow swings. The Rev Spinner has finger holes only, no thumb, and is released onto carpet to spin in place; engraved bars on the base let you count how long it spins. A longer spin is a rough cue that more finger lift went into the release. The bars are a practice cue only. The disc does not measure your rev rate.

**8. I'm a two-handed bowler.**
The Rev Spinner is your product. The Rev Puck is for thumb-in bowlers.

**9. Will it add revs or raise my average?**
We do not know. To our knowledge no study has measured what an off-lane trainer does to rev rate or average, and we will not quote a number until our own pilot has produced one. We plan to run that pilot and publish what it finds, whatever it says. Here is what we can say. Coaches agree the thumb must leave first and a relaxed hand creates more revs. The drills are designed to practice a relaxed fingertip hold and a clean, early thumb exit, in your real fit. The 30-day fit guarantee covers the fit, not a result: if the printed holes miss your sheet, we reprint.

**10. How do I use it, and how often?**
Six drills, 10 to 15 minutes a day. Rev Puck: fingertip carries (5 holds of 30 seconds, thumb loose), thumb pops (3 sets of 10, wrist cord on), slow shadow swings, and cupped-wrist holds (5 holds of 20 seconds). Rev Spinner: carpet spins, counting the seconds, and the rotation-axis drill. Short daily sessions help keep the feel between league nights. Stop if anything hurts. The full guide ships with every order.

**11. Why about 300 grams?**
Light enough for long holds, heavy enough to need real fingertip pressure. That is the weight in PETG at 20% infill. When it gets easy, add coins or washers to the weight pocket in the base and seal it with tape; about 100 g of quarters fit.

**12. What is it made of?**
PETG, printed with four walls and 20% infill, flat side down, with the holes lightly sanded. PETG is tougher than PLA at the thumb bore, which is why we use it. PETG softens near 80 °C, so keep it out of hot cars.

**13. Can I throw it?**
No. It is a swing-and-hold and carpet-release tool. It is never thrown, at a wall, a pin or anything else. For swing drills, loop the wrist cord around your wrist.

**14. My fit changes. What then?**
So does the file. Your sheet is stored with your order. Get the new sheet from your pro shop, enter it, and we reprint. We quote the reprint price before we print.

**15. What do I get with the digital files?**
Your Rev Puck file ($29) or Rev Puck and Rev Spinner files ($39), generated from your drill sheet, with your fit sheet and our print settings: PETG, four walls, 20% gyroid infill, flat side down, no supports. Print on your own printer or at a print shop. Printed hole sizes vary by printer, so use the configurator's sizer plate to test your printer first and finish the fit with tape. The 30-day fit guarantee does not cover files you print yourself.

**16. Left-handed?**
Yes. Select left hand and the layout and lateral pitches mirror.

**17. Can my pro shop order for me?**
Yes. Participating shops enter your sheet at the counter, we print and drop-ship, and the shop earns 25% of the order. Ask your shop, or send us the shop's name and we will reach out.

**18. Is it approved by a governing body or tour?**
It is a training aid used off the lane. It is not a bowling ball and is never used in sanctioned play. To our knowledge no governing-body approval applies to a product like this, and we claim none.

**19. What is the guarantee and return policy?**
Every trainer is made to order from your sheet, so returns on custom goods are limited, which is standard for personalized products. The 30-day fit guarantee is simple: if the printed holes do not match the sheet you entered, we reprint at no charge. Your sheet is on file so we can check. The guarantee covers parts we print, not digital files printed elsewhere.

**20. Is my drill sheet private?**
Your fit sheet is personal data tied to your hand. We store it with your order so we can reprint, we state how long we keep it, and we delete it on request.

## 8. Objection-handling scripts

Format: the objection as the customer says it, the answer in the words we use, the fact that backs it up, and the next step. Say the answer; do not argue the objection.

### 1. "I can just hold my ball."

**Answer:** You can, for about as long as your forearm lasts. Your ball is fifteen pounds and drilled to be thrown, and you cannot drop-catch it to practice the thumb exit in your living room. The puck is about 300 grams in PETG at 20% infill, built from your drill sheet, so you can hold it for a 30-second carry, pop the thumb out while the fingers catch it, and put it in your bag.
**Fact behind it:** About 300 g in PETG at 20% infill; same hole geometry; wrist-cord tunnel.
**Next step:** "Type your sheet into the configurator and look at it. It takes a minute."

### 2. "Does it actually add revs?"

**Answer:** Honest answer: to our knowledge nobody has measured what any off-lane trainer does to rev rate, including us, so I will not give you a number. What I can tell you is that coaches agree the release depends on a relaxed hand and a clean, early thumb exit, and the drills are designed to practice those two things in your real fit. We plan to run a pilot and publish what it finds, whatever it says.
**Fact behind it:** Attributed coaching consensus; no controlled study that we know of; pilot planned.
**Next step:** "There is a 30-day fit guarantee. If the printed holes miss your sheet, we reprint."

### 3. "It's $89 for a piece of printed plastic."

**Answer:** It is $89 for a trainer built from your drill sheet in sixty-fourths of an inch, with both spans and every pitch, on the real ball curvature, made one at a time for your hand. By our estimate a pro shop charges in a similar range to drill a ball, and this one goes in your bag. If you want to start smaller, the Fit Kit is $24 and credited toward the trainer.
**Fact behind it:** Made to order from the sheet; drilling a ball runs roughly $40 to $150 (our estimate, low confidence; check current shop prices before saying it aloud); Fit Kit credit.

**Answer:** A brace holds your wrist for you during the shot. This is designed to let you practice that work without one. They are different tools. A brace is sold in small, medium or large. This is built from your drill sheet.
**Fact behind it:** Wrist supports are sold S/M/L; RollRead is built from the fit.

**Answer:** Good. So does the file. Your sheet is on record, and when your pro shop writes a new one, we reprint from it. We quote the reprint price before we print, and you keep the old one for comparison.

### 7. "A hand gripper does the same thing for ten dollars."

**Answer:** A gripper works your whole hand in a squeeze. Coaching sources say squeezing limits hook, and a fitted grip is meant to let you hold on without it. The puck is designed for fingertip grip holds in the exact position you bowl in, with your thumb loose and your fingers carrying the weight. At about 300 g it is mainly a feel-and-habit tool, and you can add weight to the pocket or the cord for strength work. A gripper has a different shape from your hold on the ball.
**Fact behind it:** Yellow claim with its qualifier ("designed for"); at 300 g the puck alone is a feel-and-habit tool, not a strength tool; coaching sources say oversized holes make you squeeze and squeezing limits hook.
**Next step:** "Hold it for 30 seconds with the thumb loose and the fingers carrying the weight. See how it feels."

### 8. "I'm two-handed. I don't use my thumb."

**Answer:** Then the Rev Spinner is the one for you. It has finger holes only, from your sizes, and you release it onto carpet and count the spin. The puck is for thumb-in bowlers.
**Fact behind it:** The Rev Spinner is fingers-only and sold on its own at $45.

## 10. Glossary of fit terms, in customer language

**Drill sheet.** The card your pro shop fills out when they fit you: hole sizes, spans and pitches. It is the recipe for your grip. RollRead is built from it.

**Hole size.** The diameter of each hole, written in sixty-fourths of an inch, for example 55/64 or 1 1/32. The configurator accepts fractions typed the way your shop writes them.

**1/64 inch.** The step pro shops use for hole sizes, about four tenths of a millimeter. One wrap of bowler's tape is in the same neighborhood.

**Span.** The distance from the near edge of your thumb hole to the near edge of each finger hole. You have two, middle and ring, and the middle span is usually a few millimeters longer.

**Pitch.** The angle a hole is drilled at. Zero pitch aims the hole straight at the ball's center. Forward pitch tilts a hole toward the palm side of the grip (for the fingers, toward the thumb; for the thumb, toward the fingers). Reverse pitch tilts it the other way. Lateral pitch tilts a hole left or right across the hand. Pitches are written in fractions of an inch, like 1/8 or 3/8.

**Fingertip grip.** Fingers in to the first joint. The drills in the guide are written for a fingertip grip. The configurator takes your finger depth from the sheet.

**Conventional grip.** Fingers in to the second joint, the way a house ball is drilled.

**Thumb exit.** The moment the thumb leaves the hole. Coaches agree it must leave first, before the fingers lift.

**Lift.** The fingers pulling up through the ball as it comes off the hand. The Rev Spinner is designed to give a cue on lift through how long it spins.

**Cupped wrist.** Wrist bent so the palm faces you, keeping the hand under the ball into the release.

**Squeezing.** Gripping the ball with hand pressure instead of hanging it on relaxed fingertips. Coaching sources say oversized holes make you squeeze and squeezing limits hook.

**Bowler's tape.** Textured or smooth tape placed inside the thumb hole to fine-tune the size. It works in the trainer the same way it works in your ball.

**Blind thumb / through thumb.** A blind thumb hole has a floor, like your ball. A through hole is open at the back. Both are options in the configurator.

**Bridge.** The web of material between the two finger holes.

**Weight pocket.** The recess under the Rev Puck for coins or washers, sealed with tape, for adding weight as holds get easier.

**Wrist cord.** Paracord through the tunnel in the side of the Rev Puck, looped around the wrist for every swing drill so the trainer stays tied to you.

**Print allowance.** The small extra diameter the configurator adds to every hole, because 3D-printed holes come out slightly small. Final fit is done with tape, like a ball.

**Sizer plate (Fit Kit).** A flat printed plate with labeled test bores in 1/64-inch steps around your expected sizes, printed on the same printer and material as your trainer, so the bore that fits is the bore we print.

**PETG and infill.** PETG is the plastic we print in; it is tougher than PLA at the thumb bore. Infill is the lattice inside the part. At 20% infill the Rev Puck weighs about 300 g.

**One-handed thumb-in / two-handed.** One-handed bowlers put the thumb in the ball and release with one hand. Two-handed bowlers keep the thumb out and support the ball with the other hand. The Rev Puck is for thumb-in bowlers; the Rev Spinner is for both.

**Rev rate.** How many times the ball turns over on its way down the lane, measured in revolutions per minute by apps and sensors. RollRead does not measure it and makes no claim about yours.

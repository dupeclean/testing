## 1. Launch narrative and six-week launch calendar

### 1.1 The story we tell over six weeks

The product needs one sentence of explanation before anyone wants it. The launch is built to deliver that sentence, then show the thing, then let the bowler type in their own numbers. Everything else is in service of those three steps.

The arc runs from fit to practice to proof. We open on the drill sheet, because every target customer already has one and already paid for it. We move to the sizer plate, because fit is the claim we can demonstrate on a bench. Then the drills, led by the thumb pop, because a fifteen-second clip of a puck dropping onto the fingers explains the product better than a paragraph. Then the spinner, which brings in two-handed and youth bowlers and their parents. Then the pro shop, which holds the sheet and the trust. We close on the pilot, because the one question every serious bowler will ask is the one we cannot answer yet, and saying so plainly is the brand.

Six themes, one per week, each with a hero video, a channel plan and a number to watch:

| 4 | Lift it, spin it, count it | Train the hand that bowls. | Script 8 |
| 5 | Ask your pro shop | Same holes. Same pitch. No lane fee. | Script 9 and the pro-shop counter card |
| 6 | We are measuring | Relax the thumb. Let the fingers work. | Script 10 and the pilot page |

### 9.3 Demo script (about 90 seconds)

Said in the booth's voice: plain, specific, candid. Adjust to the person.

1. **Open (5 s).** "Do you bowl one-handed, thumb in?" If yes, continue. If two-handed: "Then the disc on the carpet is yours. Want to try it?"
2. **The sentence (10 s).** "It's the part of your ball your hand touches, printed with your exact holes, so you can practice your release anywhere. It's built from your drill sheet."
3. **The plate (20 s).** Hand them a sizer plate. "Put your thumb in one. Find the bore that's snug at the first joint with no squeeze. That's the size we'd print. These step in sixty-fourths, same as your sheet." Let them find it. Say the size out loud.
4. **The puck (25 s).** Hand them the nearest default puck, cord on the wrist. "Fingers in, thumb loose. Feel how the thumb and fingers oppose each other around the curve? The holes aim at the center of an 8.5-inch ball, the way a zero-pitch hole is drilled. Yours would have your spans and your pitches." Then: "Now relax the thumb and let it slide out. Fingers catch it." Watch the drop.
5. **The honest line (15 s).** "People ask if it adds revs. We don't know; nobody has measured off-lane trainers, including us. It trains a relaxed hand and an early thumb exit in your own holes, and we're running a pilot. Thirty-day fit guarantee either way."
6. **The ask (15 s).** "Do you have your drill sheet, or does your shop? Either way, fill in this card or type it on the tablet, and we'll email you your model in 3D tonight. Everyone who completes a sheet is in today's drawing for a free Pair printed from their sheet."

### 9.4 Lead capture

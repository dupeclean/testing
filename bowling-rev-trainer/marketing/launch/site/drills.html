The drills

The same text is on the card in the box. Ten of each a day is plenty.

Setting up

  1. A strip of painter's tape on the carpet, about two meters long, with a target at the far end: a pin, a cup, anything that falls over.
  2. Kneel at the near end with the ball in your hand the way you hold your own. Thumb in, fingers in, tape in the thumb if it needs it.
  3. For the second drill, a phone on the floor behind you, propped so it looks down the tape, recording in slow motion.

Drill 1: the yo-yo roll

Hand behind the ball. Swing low and let it go with the fingers lifting straight up through it. No turn at all. The ball should run straight, long and lively down the tape and knock the target over.

Read it off the path, not the bullseye:

  • Straight and long: that's it. The hand stayed behind the ball and the fingers lifted.
  • Straight and short: a dump. The thumb and fingers came out together, no lift.
  • A hop off the carpet: the thumb hung on and popped it up.
  • It curved: you turned it. Fine for drill 2, not for this one.

Drill 2: the proper release

Same swing. Now the thumb leaves first, the fingers lift, and the hand turns through the ball the way you do on the lane. The ball rolls and curves off the line. Pick a target one or two steps over from the straight one and aim to knock that one down.

Now the phone. At the moment of release the track ring shows as an oval. The more you turn through the ball, the wider it opens. The card has a scale: a ring that's about half open is 30 degrees of axis rotation, three quarters open is 45. If the oval leans, that's axis tilt. Don't chase a number. Chase the same oval, the same curve and the same target, ten in a row. Add speed and revs after that holds, not before.

The bullseye

The two bullseyes mark your axis point. When the ball spins about that axis they hold still, and on carpet you'll see that in the first frames of the slow-motion clip. After that the carpet grabs the ball and swings a hook's axis round, so the bullseye wheels in a circle. The size of that circle is your rotation. Same circle every time is the goal, same as the oval.

A target that tells you which way you missed

The release gate is a row of loose pins on a strip of tape, a step and a half past where the ball is let go. The center pin is the yo-yo target; one or two over is the hook target. The wrong pin falling says which way the release missed. It's a free print file in the configurator. Once mine has proved itself on my own floor it goes in the box with every ball.

Rev Puck drills

  • Fingertip carries. Hold it by the fingers only, thumb out, as long as you can without the wrist breaking. At your desk, on the couch.
  • Thumb pops. Cord on the wrist. A slow swing, and at the bottom the thumb comes out clean while the fingers stay. The puck swings on the cord. Feel where the thumb left.
  • Swing sync. The same slow swing with your full approach timing, no release, feeling the thumb go loose at the bottom.
  • Cupped-wrist holds. Cup it and hold. Add quarters to the pocket when it gets easy.

Rev Spinner drills

  • Carpet spins. Short pendulum, lift at the bottom, let it go onto the carpet. Count the flicker of the bars until it stops. More lift, longer count. Same count ten times running is the goal, not the biggest number.
  • Axis control. Same release, and watch whether it spins flat or wobbles. A wobble is the hand turning unevenly through the release.

None of this measures your rev rate, and I make no promise about changing it. These are feedback tools: they show you which release you did, so you can do the same one on purpose.

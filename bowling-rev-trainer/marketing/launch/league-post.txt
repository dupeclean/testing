This one's for the one-handed, thumb-in crowd. Your shop measured your hand to a sixty-fourth and drilled your spans and pitches into your ball. That fit lives in one place: the ball. So there's no working on your release at home unless you're swinging fifteen pounds across the living room.

I built a practice ball that takes the fit off the ball. It's 3D printed from your drill sheet: hole sizes in 1/64-inch steps, both spans, every pitch, right or left hand. It's 200 mm across and about 700 g, smaller and a lot lighter than your ball, and you roll it on carpet from a kneeling swing along a strip of tape. Two bullseyes mark your axis point and a ring runs where your track is. On a clean roll the bullseye sits still and the ring stays round. Turn through it like you do on the lane and the ring opens into an oval and the ball curves off the tape. You can see your release in the living room, ten rolls a night, and know which one you did.

It's a feedback tool, not a magic ball. I won't tell you it adds revs. It shows you what your hand did.

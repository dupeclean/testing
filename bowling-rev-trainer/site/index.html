Rev Puck. The part you hold.

A slice of ball surface the size of your hand, with your thumb, middle and ring holes drilled toward the ball's center with your pitches applied, exactly as a pro shop aims them. A fingers-only disc cut from the same ball surface, with your middle and ring hole sizes, drilled straight at the ball's center. Coaches agree on where revs come from. FitSlice lets you practice it with your real fit.

Coaches agree the thumb must leave first, and a relaxed hand creates more revs. Oversized holes make you squeeze; squeezing limits hook. Those are coaching views, and they are the reason a thumb-in bowler's fit matters so much: the pro-shop trade body puts hole size at about 20% of a fit, with span and pitch at 40% each. We copy all three.

A relaxed grip under load

Hanging the Rev Puck on your fingertips with the thumb loose is designed for fingertip holds in the exact position you bowl in; hang a weight from the cord when you want strength work. If your thumb knuckle whitens, you are squeezing. Reset and go again.

A clean, early thumb exit

Practice a clean, early thumb exit with the thumb-pop drill: relax the thumb, let it slide out, and let the fingers catch the weight. If the puck falls, the fingers were not carrying it. You cannot drill that safely with a fifteen-pound ball in your living room.

Finger lift you can count

Release the Rev Spinner onto carpet and count how long it spins. Try to beat your count.

Questions bowlers ask first.

Does it feel like my ball?
The holes are your sizes, at your spans, with your pitches, on the same 8.5" radius, drilled toward the same center. Your index and pinky rest on the curve. The first thing most people do is check the thumb. Check the thumb.
Does it add revs?
We do not know, and nobody else does either, because no study has measured any off-lane trainer. What it does is let you practice, with your real fit, the two things coaches agree the release depends on: a relaxed hand and an early, clean thumb exit.
I bowl two-handed.
The Rev Spinner is your tool. It needs only your finger sizes.
My fit changes.
So does your file. Your sheet is stored with your order. A reprint with the new sheet costs less than a re-drill.
Do I need a 3D printer?
No. We print and ship. If you have a printer, the Digital Files are $29 for the puck and $39 for the pair.
What if I type a number wrong?
The 3D preview draws every hole axis so a reversed pitch is visible before you order. The Fit Kit confirms hole sizes on the actual printer. And if the part does not match your sheet, we reprint it.
